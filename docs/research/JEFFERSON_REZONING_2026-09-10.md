# Jefferson County: standard rezoning research pilot

Prepared September 10, 2026. **Research sample, not a complete requirement count or determination of current applicable law.** The accompanying [combined evidence study](../../research/land_use/rezoning-2026-09-10.json) contains 20 scoped observations and their exact excerpts, physical PDF pages, printed pages, sections, source URLs, preserved originals and SHA-256 values. The offline study validator reproduces the source-hash and excerpt checks.

## Comparable scenario and boundaries

A private owner of an existing parcel in **unincorporated Jefferson County** seeks a **change to a standard zoning district** because the assumed proposed use is not allowed by the existing zoning. This assumption must be checked against a real parcel before using the process. The comparable process is zoning-map amendment, through county decision and resolution recording, rather than complete permission to construct or occupy a project.

The principal path is Section 6, with community meeting requirements in Section 5, notification in Section 3, and the **“Rezoning to Standard Zone District” column** of Section 9. Section 4 pre-application review is **optional**. A subdivision, planned development, Special Use, variance, prior denial or litigation, enforcement correction, public-facility proposal, and amendment of designated PD open space are outside the assumed scenario. Those circumstances can change the process. Section 8 Location and Extent concerns public buildings/facilities/uses and public utilities, including privately owned public utilities; private ownership alone does not establish its applicability. [Zoning Resolution, PDF pp. 23, 26, 42, 47](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/1828).

Later site development, building, grading, fire, water, wastewater, access and other approvals are not assumed unnecessary. This pilot does not fully decompose the Land Development Regulation incorporated into the Section 9 water, wastewater and environmental submissions. Parcel-specific zone restrictions, plans, overlays and the new Wildland Urban Interface boundary require a separate applicability check.

For JEF-RZ-07 through 13, Section 9.B expressly preserves reuse of previously approved documents that remain applicable and compliant under consistent circumstances, discretionary waiver of documents that would not materially aid review, and authority to request additional documents for the particular application. The table's **R** means normally required; **A** means potentially required by the Case Manager. These distinctions rule out counting every table row as mandatory for every rezoning. [Zoning Resolution, PDF pp.46–48](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/1828#page=46).

The sampled observations are intentionally incomplete. Examples outside the 20-row sample include first-referral and hearing notices, newspaper notice, applicant hearing-document submission, some resubmittal requirements, conditional studies and later resolution recording. Context evidence preserves several of these passages without presenting the sample size as the county's total burden.

## Fees and timing that must remain qualified

**Fee formula:** `max(400 × sqrt(acres), 900)` USD, plus the applicable $75 community-meeting and $400 combined-hearing charges. The PDF's square-root sign is visually confirmed; dropping it would materially overstate the fee. These are selected county process charges, not a total development cost. Additional agency review, postage/materials, studies, later permits, credits, waivers and special facts can change the amount. [2026 fee table, PDF p.1](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/15930/#page=1).

**Public Health:** the posted rezoning review amount is $350. The schedule alone does not prove that every standard rezoning is referred to Public Health. Section 9.C.2.b requires payment of review fees charged by a referral agency; preserve that trigger. By contrast, the planning fee table explicitly states that **all optional preliminary reviews go to Public Health**: its $350 base is accompanied by the posted $275 Public Health preliminary-application fee. Policy C.1 permits the preliminary development application fee to be applied toward formal submission within one year; no automatic credit of the separate Public Health fee is established here. [Public Health schedule](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/51106/), [fee table](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/15930/), [fee policy, PDF p.2](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/320/Development-Application-and-Permit-Fees-Policy-PDF#page=2).

The posted third and subsequent referral resubmittal charge is $500. Additional referrals are conditional: the Case Manager can allow a substantially conforming first-referral application with only minor revisions to proceed directly to hearing documents. Qualified affordable-housing waivers/proration, discretionary fee waiver, and violation-correction double fees appear in the policy; none is assumed for the scenario. [Fee table, PDF p.1](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/15930/#page=1), [Zoning Resolution, PDF p.27](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/1828#page=27), [fee policy](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/320/Development-Application-and-Permit-Fees-Policy-PDF).

**Timing:** the zoning text estimates **106 calendar days from first referral to the public-hearing phase**, conditional on completeness and meeting the described intervals. The administrative guide gives a **5–6 month staff goal including pre-application**, with different start/end boundaries. Neither is an empirical average, guaranteed maximum, or complete entitlement schedule. Section 6.A allows time-frame waivers based on staffing and complexity. The 21-day first-referral applicant response preserves the example hearing date; the distinct 180-day withdrawal threshold can be extended for good cause. [Zoning Resolution, PDF pp.26–28](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/1828#page=26), [Rezoning guide, PDF p.4](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/9504#page=4).

For a standard-zone approval, the BCC resolution is to be recorded within seven calendar days after approval. The 28-day clean ODP/SUD and recordation-fee provision concerns planned-development or Special Use documents and should not be assigned to the standard-zone scenario. [Zoning Resolution, PDF p.29](https://www.jeffco.us/DocumentCenter/View/1828#page=29).

## Verification and next use

All 12 originals retain their requested/final URL, retrieval timestamp, exact bytes and SHA-256. The 20 observations and their retained context contain page-exact PDF citations, validated after Unicode NFKC and whitespace normalization. Tables, the radical fee formula, relevant procedures and exception passages were rendered and visually reviewed. The guide supplies one short quotation solely for its staff goal; adopted text supplies the requirement excerpts. No original source bytes were edited and no observations were promoted into canonical legal-rule records.

Before a parcel-specific comparison or cost estimate, confirm the requested zoning change, acreage, Mountains/Plains location, overlays, current applicable amendments and incorporated standards; determine conditional submissions/referrals and credit/waiver eligibility; then collect actual case-processing times. This pilot supports an auditable process comparison and identifies what still needs verification, without measuring all regulatory burden.
