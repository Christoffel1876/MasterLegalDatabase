Any broker who receives money belonging to others must establish written accounting 
control policies and procedures, which must include adequate checks and balances over 
the financial activities of the broker while managing the risk of fraud or illegal acts.91 
 
There are multiple classifications of brokers: 
 
• Independent Brokers, 
• Associate Brokers, 
• Supervising Brokers, 
• Employing Brokers, and 
• Brokerage Firms. 
 
Independent Brokers act as their own brokerage firms or sole proprietors, and do not 
employ or supervise any Associate Broker.92 An Associate Broker is an Individual Broker 
that works under the supervision of an Employing Broker.93 Employing Brokers are those 
who have the option to employ or engage other brokers.94 An Employing Broker can 
delegate duties to Supervisory Brokers. 95 Finally, a Brokerage Firm refers to any 
business entity authorized by the Commission to perform real estate services. All 
Brokerage Firms must have an active Employing Broker if they choose to employ other 
licensees.96  
 
A broker cannot act as a Brokerage Firm without first having served actively as a real 
estate broker for at least two years.97 No such broker shall employ another broker 
without also first having completed 24 clock hours of instruction in brokerage 
administration.98 They need to also demonstrate experience and knowledge sufficient 
to enable the broker to employ and adequately supervise others. The Commission must 
adopt rules by which the broker may demonstrate such experience, either by 
documenting a specified number of transactions that the broker has completed or by 
other methods.99 These rules can be found in 4 CCR 725-1-2.5.
