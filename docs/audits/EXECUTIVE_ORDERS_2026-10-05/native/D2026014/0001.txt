D 2026 014 
EXECUTIVE ORDER 
Extending Executive Order D 2026 009 Declaring a Disaster Emergency Due to the Aspen 
Acres and Gold Mountain Fires in Colorado, Temporarily Suspending Certain Statutes 
and Regulations, and Authorizing Employment of the Colorado National Guard 
Pursuant to the authority vested in the Governor of the State of Colorado and, in 
particular, pursuant to Article IV, Section 2, of the Colorado Constitution and the relevant 
portions of the Colorado Disaster Emergency Act, C.R.S. § 24-33.5-701, et seq., I, Jared Polis, 
Governor of the State of Colorado, hereby issue this Executive Order extending Executive Order 
D 2026 009 declaring a state of disaster emergency due to the Aspen Acres Fire in Custer and 
Pueblo Counties and the Gold Mountain Fire in Ouray County.  
Pursuant to C.R.S. § 24-33.5-704(7)(a), I temporarily suspend the requirements of C.R.S. 
§ 25-3.5-202, § 25-3.5-203, and § 25-3.5-206 and corresponding regulations in 6 CCR 1015-3, 
Chapter 2 for currently certified or licensed out-of-state Emergency Medical Service (EMS) 
providers. The suspensions allow EMS providers from non-Compact states who are requested 
through the Colorado Department of Public Safety (CDPS) incident command structure to assist 
in wildfire response, and who are credentialed to practice under the co-direction of their home 
state medical director and a Colorado medical director to operate within the incident command 
structure.  
Further, pursuant to Article IV, Section 5 of the Colorado Constitution and C.R.S. § 28-3-
104, I authorize employing unarmed members of the Colorado National Guard for the purpose of 
supporting fire suppression and response efforts, as deemed necessary by CDPS for the Aspen 
Acres and Gold Mountain Fires. 
 
Background and Purpose 
The Governor is responsible for meeting the dangers to the State and people presented by 
disasters. C.R.S. § 24-33.5-704(1). The Colorado Disaster Emergency Act defines a disaster as 
“the occurrence or imminent threat of widespread or severe damage, injury, or loss of life or 
property resulting from any natural cause or cause of human origin, including but not limited to 
fire.” C.R.S. § 24-33.5-703(3).
