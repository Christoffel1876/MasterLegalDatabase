On July 24, 2026, I issued Executive Order D 2026 009, which memorialized these 
verbal declarations and extended these declarations for an additional thirty days due to ongoing 
need for fire suppression, response, consequence management, mitigation and recovery efforts.  
The burn scar from the Aspen Acres Fire caused a loss of vegetation and hydrophobic 
soil which led to an increase in runoff and debris flow throughout the area. As a result, on July 
28, 29, 30, August 16, and 19 rains around the burn scar created flooding in Pueblo and Custer 
counties. Homes in Rye, Colorado City, Beulah, and San Isabel have been damaged by flooding. 
Roads, culverts, and bridges have also been eroded and undercut. On August 16, roughly 1 to 1.5 
inches of rain fell over the Beulah area in 90 minutes. Due to the significant burn scar from the 
Aspen Acres Fire, flash flooding occurred and escalated rapidly. Tragically, a Beulah resident 
lost his life in the flood waters. 
On July 26, heavy rainfall caused debris flows from the Gold Mountain Fire burn scar, 
resulting in rock slides and road closures, including State Highway 550. Private property was 
damaged. On July 28, 2026, additional intense rain produced flash flooding and debris flow 
which damaged county roads, residential property, and critical infrastructure, including electric, 
water, and natural gas facilities. Within the Town of Ouray, debris flows closed streets. 
Modeling has shown that up to 150 homes are at risk for future flooding and debris flow from as 
little as 0.4 inches of rain. 
With the severity of the flooding and additional rain expected in the forecast, it is 
necessary to extend Executive Order D 2026 009 to allow for the State of Colorado Emergency 
Operations Plan and the Office of Emergency Management to take all necessary and appropriate 
State action to assist the affected area with consequence management, mitigation, and recovery 
efforts.  
With the impact these fires and subsequent flooding have had on the State, it may be that 
EMS workforce outside of the State will be needed to assist in response and recovery efforts.  
Therefore, I extend the temporary suspension of the requirements of C.R.S. § 25-3.5-202, § 25-
3.5-203, and § 25-3.5-206 and corresponding regulations in 6 CCR 1015-3, Chapter 2 for 
currently certified or licensed out-of-state EMS providers. The suspensions of these statutes and 
regulations will allow for more flexibility for qualified non-Compact state EMS providers to help 
CDPS with response and recovery efforts, especially if there is a need greater than our in-State 
workforce.  
Similarly, I extend authorization of employing unarmed members of the Colorado 
National Guard for the purpose of supporting response and recovery efforts, as deemed necessary 
by CDPS pursuant to Article IV, Section 5 of the Colorado Constitution and C.R.S. § 28-3-104.
