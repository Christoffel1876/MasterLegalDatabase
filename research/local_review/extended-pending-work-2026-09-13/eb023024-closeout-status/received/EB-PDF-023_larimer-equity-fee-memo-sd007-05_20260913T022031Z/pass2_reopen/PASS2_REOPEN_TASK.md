### Challenges list (continuation from page 1 Background)

Pass2 captions report four bullets:

1. The new fee structure created many new fee categories, which did not easily align to the County’s existing accounting structures and **Energov system**.  
2. Some of the fee categories are duplicative or overlapping, which has created confusion for customers and made it difficult for staff to consistently interpret and administer.  
3. Some fee amounts are very close to one another (e.g., **$5 to $10 difference**) for similar services, which introduces unnecessary complexity.  
4. The “**equity fee reduction**” option has been beneficial for several projects. However, the resolution, as written, does not account for all situations where such a fee reduction could be beneficial.

### Recommended Changes to Fee Schedule (underlined heading)

**Lead paragraph:**  
Implementation of the full (**100%**) fee amounts is scheduled to take effect **January 2, 2024**. To simplify implementation and improve the customer experience, staff recommend several minor adjustments to the fee schedule. Recommendations include:

**Bullets:**

1. Combine fee categories that are very similar in nature. In the case where the fees may differ slightly, use averaging or cost recovery data to determine the most appropriate fee amount.  
2. Round fees up or down to the nearest **$5 increment** to simplify customer communication and understanding.  
3. Eliminate fees that are duplicative or unnecessary.  
4. Add the **Consumer Price Index (CPI)** factor at the same time building permit fees are adjusted in **July 2024** (rather than January).  
5. Reorganize fee structure to improve the ease of use.

**Deferred change paragraph:**  
At the November 20 work session, staff proposed a change to absorb the cost of meeting publication and notification letters into each fee category, rather than calculating those fees on a project-by-project basis. While generally supported by the Commissioners, additional review and analysis is needed to implement this change, so it will be reconsidered at a future date.

The Resolution is set to expire on **December 31, 2023**.
