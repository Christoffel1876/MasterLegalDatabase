### Recorder stamp: upper margin

The punctuation-light visible reading is:

```text
312315 04/24/2024 01 37 PM B 1056 P 691 ORDIN
Page 1 of 2 R $0 00 D $0 00 T $0 00 Clear Creek
```

Reception `312315`, date `04/24/2024`, time components `01 37 PM`, book `1056`,
page `691`, `ORDIN`, and `Page 1 of 2` are supported. Three monetary fields labeled
R, D, and T visibly show zero values. A barcode graphic is present and was not
decoded. The crop does not establish visible colons after B/P or between the time
digits, or decimal dots in the zero amounts. PASS1/PASS2's `01:37`, `B:`, `P:`, and
`$0.00` are understandable normalization, not exact source punctuation.

This is recording metadata, not proof of adoption, effectiveness, newspaper
publication, or that every fee under the ordinance is zero. The candidate's
`Bage 1 01/2/2924` and following mixed-script string do not faithfully transcribe
the stamp or barcode.

### Third WHEREAS: middle of page

```text
WHEREAS, the Clear Creek Fire Authority recommends adoption of the 2018 edition of
the Uniform Fire Code, and
```

`2018` is clear, not the candidate's `2013`. The source itself says **Uniform Fire
Code**; do not silently substitute another code title because the edition/title
combination appears unusual. The recital says “recommends adoption”; this sentence
alone does not enact that recommended code. The following recital ends
`repeal Ordinance No 6`, with no terminal period apparent.

### Repeal and effectiveness clause

```text
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Board of County Commissioners for
Clear Creek County, that Ordinance No 6 is hereby repealed in full effective immediately upon
adoption
```

The candidate's `immedrately` should be `immediately`. Preserve the absence of a
period after `No` and the apparent absence of terminal punctuation after
`adoption`. This is the clause's stated legal effect, conditional on adoption;
page 1's first-reading block alone does not establish the completed adoption date
or whether this instrument remains operative now.

### Publication directive

```text
BE IT EVEN FURTHER ORDAINED by the Board of County Commissioners that,
upon adoption, this Ordinance shall be published by title only in a newspaper(s) of general
circulation in the County
```

`EVEN FURTHER` and the unusual literal `a newspaper(s)` are source wording.
No terminal period is apparent after `County`. Preserve this title-only directive
separately from the first-reading block's order for publication **in full**; do
not harmonize the two statements or claim that either proves completed publication.

### First reading and certification

```text
INTRODUCED, FIRST READ, ORDERED PUBLISHED IN FULL AND SET FOR
PUBLIC HEARING THIS 19th DAY OF March, 2024
```

The day is `19` with raised `th`; `19th` is a plain-text representation of that
layout. The day and `March` are typeset fills on rules, not freehand date entries.
There is a comma between March and 2024; no terminal period is apparent. The
candidate's `‹19"`, detached `DAY OF.`, and `March 2024` lose the suffix and
association, and introduce an unsupported period after `OF`.

The certification reads:

```text
CERTIFICATION THE FOREGOING ORDINANCE WAS INTRODUCED AND
READ ON March 19, 2024, BY THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF
CLEAR CREEK COUNTY, COLORADO, AND APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION
```

`March 19` is another typeset fill on a rule. No colon after `CERTIFICATION` or
terminal punctuation after `PUBLICATION` is apparent. This is first-reading and
approval-for-publication language, not a final-adoption certification.
